How to read these numbers

Pace divides transcript word count by the entire recording length. Bible readings, prayers, silence, and other voices can affect it. Compare recordings of the same type for a more useful trend.

Verbal clutter per 1,000 words counts exact, case-insensitive occurrences of “you know,” “I mean,” “kind of,” “sort of,” “basically,” “actually,” “really,” “just,” “um,” “uh,” and “er” in available transcripts. These are cues for listening review, not errors or a quality score. Each may be appropriate in context, and automated transcripts can omit spoken sounds or misrecognize words. The most repeated selected phrase is shown for each recording. Expand a recording to review all selected phrase counts, rates, and short transcript examples. A dash means no transcript was available or no listed cue was found.

Use a trend to choose a recording to listen to, then review a short passage for pace, clarity, and wording habits. Research: filler frequency and audience ratings; fillers and recall; ASR disfluency limitations.
